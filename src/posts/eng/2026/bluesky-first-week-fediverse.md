A few days ago I wrote about [Mastodon, Bluesky and the language problem](/posts/mastodon-bluesky-english/) and ended it with a half-baked idea, Italian on Mastodon and English on Bluesky... well, I actually did it!

Truth is, I opened my **Bluesky** account last year, grabbed the handle and then... never really used it. So this is honestly **my first week**: only in the past few days have I started using it a lot (and I mean _a lot_), trying to build myself a little English-speaking corner over there, while the **fediverse** (Mastodon, in my case) stays the place where I ramble in Italian. Anyway, here are my notes from week one: setting things up with **Bridgy Fed**, getting lost in **custom feeds** and **starter packs**, and what talking to people on Bluesky feels like for someone who's living on Mastodon since years.

If you're coming from Bluesky, hi! If you're coming from the fediverse, hi to you too, and in a minute you'll see why you can read and reply to this as well

My account is a native Bluesky one, not bridged from somewhere else: it's [@andreacorinti.com](https://bsky.app/profile/andreacorinti.com), with this very domain as the handle (which still feels a bit like having your name on the doorbell of the internet, eh?).

to bridge a Bluesky profile to the fediverse you just follow `@ap.brid.gy` and that's it, people on Mastodon & co. can now find me as `@andreacorinti.com@bsky.brid.gy` and their replies come back to Bluesky.

## Bluesky custom feeds, trends and the algorithm question

This is the biggest difference with Mastodon, and between me and you? The one I find most interesting.

**Custom feeds**: anyone can create a themed feed (Linux, JRPGs, retro gaming, IndieWeb…) that picks up posts based on keywords and hashtags, so you subscribe to one and suddenly you're reading people talking about the stuff you care about, whether you follow them or not, and it works the other way around too, since a post of mine mentioning "JRPG" can end up in front of people with the same obsession. On Mastodon finding interesting stuff is a side quest with no map (you follow hashtags, sure, but you only see what your server happens to know about), here custom feeds are more like fast travel points!

![Screenshot of the "JRPGs" custom feed on Bluesky showing my post "Joining the grid party!" with my "My Game of the Year (1991-2026)" grid: Monkey Island 2, Final Fantasy VII, VIII and IX, Morrowind, Persona 3 Reload and many more covers, one per year](/img/bluesky-first-week/feed-jrpg.jpg)

_my "Game of the Year 1991-2026" grid passing through the "JRPGs" custom feed (for the record, the grids are everywhere this week!)_

**Trends** work differently too, they're not based on hashtags but on groups of posts using the same words.

Then there's the algorithm: the For You feed learns mostly from your likes, while on Discover you can tell it "show more" or "show less" of something. A while ago, in my post [Di nuovo su mastodon.social?](/posts/di-nuovo-su-mastodon-social/) (in Italian), I admitted that I wouldn't mind a feed tailored to me, as long as there were no weird sponsorships and no data sold to the highest bidder, and on Bluesky, at least for now, there's **no paid promotion at all**.

A couple more things I never had on Mastodon:

- **starter packs**, lists of accounts you can follow in one go, really handy to jump straight into a niche;
- **reply controls** (the "who can reply" button, technically called _threadgates_): when you post you can decide if everybody can reply, nobody, only people you follow or only the ones you mention, and you can change it later too, handy when a thread attracts the wrong crowd! On Mastodon you can only choose who _sees_ a post, not who can answer it.

## Talking to people: threads on Bluesky vs Mastodon

_But._

What surprised me the most wasn't a feature, it was how conversations _feel_.

On Mastodon a thread is something you see _from your server_: depending on where you are some replies are simply missing, and you often have to open the original post on its home instance to get the full picture. On Bluesky **everyone sees the same thread, whole**, and that changes things way more than I expected: jumping into someone else's conversation feels natural again, a bit like on the old forums I keep saying I miss (yes, I know, I say it in every post...), and it's mostly there, in the replies more than in my own posts, that I found people worth talking to.

## The not-so-great parts of Bluesky

It's not all fast travel and alt text, of course.

First, follow-for-follow and spam, lots of it: "lofi girl" accounts, authors following 90,000 people, plain bots... at some point I did some cleaning and removed them, I'd rather have a few real people around than a crowd of ghosts! Funny enough, that's something I almost never had to deal on Mastodon, where smaller servers and human moderators keep most of it out.

Then there are the chain posts, like the "tonight you'll be visited by three spirits" one, which are kind of fun, don't get me wrong! But (my own limit, I know) like anything meme-ish, after the fourth or fifth time I see the same one in my timeline I just can't take it anymore

Then a funny one, for a guy who wrote a whole post about the language problem: **you can't change a post's language tag after publishing it**, so when one of my posts ended up tagged as "it" by mistake the only way to fix it was to delete it and post it again (the irony!).

The US side of things is huge, too, and this is the classic thing you don't think about before: time zones matter (the Italian evening is the American afternoon, so that's when conversations happen), and Discover was full of American politics, which I had to tame with a whole lot of "show less".

## Bluesky vs Mastodon and the fediverse, for now

I've already written about why Mastodon doesn't really win me over as a place to hang out, and why I stay anyway (the "together for the kids" thing, where the kids are open source and decentralisation), in that same post about the language problem, so I won't repeat it here.

Funny thing, though: I found Knowember through Bluesky, even though Robert Birming posts from a Mastodon server, and in more than four years on the fediverse our paths never crossed, not even once! That's my main issue with it, really: **without some kind of discovery, good people just stay invisible.**

And then there's another thing I've always struggled with on Mastodon: lots of people (totally legitimately!) choose to have their posts automatically deleted after a few days or months, making them kind of **biodegradable**. The right to be forgotten is sacrosanct, and there's something beautiful about it too, but I've always had a lot of trouble with this... not so much because I use the fediverse for the comments here, and I link threads far and wide that later vanish or end up all mutilated, but because I've always been a bit of a **digital archaeologist**, and every now and then I go back and reread some old forum exchanges from 2007, which luckily still survive in some backup somewhere!

Look, I understand the argument about resources spent preserving stuff that's hardly memorable, but text is really cheap:

 **a post is a kilobyte or two, while a ten-minute YouTube video in 1080p easily takes a couple hundred megabytes, which is something like a hundred thousand posts, for _one_ (probably ugly) video.**

And then there's the old idea, from Marc Bloch onwards, that history shouldn't only be about crowned heads and big events, but also about everyday lives and small exchanges, which is exactly what a timeline is made of (or at least it should be).

_this blog in 2015, Google+ button included: nobody will ever write a history book about it, but I'm glad it's still around!_

As far as I know (and I hope I'm not wrong!), Bluesky doesn't have a built-in feature like that: there are third-party tools that do it (like [SkySweeper](https://github.com/pojntfx/skysweeper) or [skeeter-deleter](https://github.com/Gorcenski/skeeter-deleter)), so whoever really wants it can still have it, but it's **not a checkbox right there in the settings**, and personally I belive that's a good thing, exactly for all the reasons above

To be fair, I still like Mastodon's sober interface, and search got a lot better with version 5.0 (I wrote about that too, in the same post about going back to mastodon.social), so for now the split works: **Italian over there, English here, and the blog in the middle** collecting replies from both.

So, that's **week one**! If you think I'm doing it all wrong, replies from Bluesky and from the fediverse both end up right under this post, so pick your side... or both, like me
