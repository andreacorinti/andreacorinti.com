It's all [Zel](https://bsky.app/profile/matteozel.bsky.social) and [Efi](https://bsky.app/profile/effimera.bsky.social)'s fault, who sent me ahead as the guinea pig (thanks guys, really!), like the poor party member you push first into the dark room to check for traps... no traps in the end, but not much treasure either

Anyway, [Blade Runner 2099](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/entertainment/blade-runner-2099-prime-video) landed on Prime Video on November 25 with all eight episodes at once and I watched exactly one, so don't take this as a review, eh, it's just the impressions of a guy who loves Blade Runner and that, boh, never really felt like the show was talking to him

## A cosplay of the future

Visually it's faithful, maybe even _too_ faithful, and between me and you? That's exactly why it feels a bit glued-on to me:

it's like a cosplay of a future imagined in 1982, a future that's long gone by now, at least the way they pictured it back then. Not ugly, don't get me wrong! It just doesn't have a personality of its own, it's a bit like a brand new hair metal band pretending it's still 1985... and I say this as a fan of Cobra Spell and Enforcer, so I'm not exactly allergic to the genre, eh.

Same story for the music by Leopold Ross, which is not bad at all, but at the end it's a Vangelis cosplay too and I'm not so sure that's what I want from a new Blade Runner...

## Michelle Yeoh (and everybody else)

Michelle Yeoh is tough, charismatic and holds every single scene she's in (but did we have any doubt?), while about the rest of the cast I won't say much, they left me pretty indifferent

## 2049 is still the one

Villeneuve's _Blade Runner 2049_ wasn't a masterpiece, but it was a damn good movie, and after this first episode it's still by far the most interesting thing anyone has done on the nostalgia-and-revival front, because it took the original and built something of its own on top of it.

Then there's the film vs series thing, which would need a whole post (one day, maybe!): a two-hour movie and an eight-episode show are different animals, and if Blade Runner's atmosphere can hold for two hours, I'm not so sure it can hold for eight episodes.

And it's funny, because in this period I'm watching [_The Ramparts of Ice_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ramparts_of_Ice), an anime, and like it always happens to me when I find an anime I like I'm left shocked by how much beauty they can squeeze in twenty minutes or so, while the 50 minutes series sometimes seem more busy making minutes than anything else (not always, eh, actually in certain contexts I would have wanted more, much more... but anyway)

so maybe for some things it would be better to stay on movies and let series go

## The Highlander effect

The gap reminded me of when I watched the _Highlander_ TV series with Duncan MacLeod and kept comparing it with the Christopher Lambert movies...

but the truth is that I liked that show a lot, with all its limits! It was the '90s, guys, TV series were what they were and nobody expected much more from them, me included, so back then you could forgive it, while today from a show with this budget and this name on it you expect more (or at least I do, which probably says more about me getting old than about the show, I know).

## Dropped, for now

I'm not crying disaster, but time is short, entertainment is endless, and with all the love I can feel for Blade Runner there isn't enough here to make me try more episodes. For now I'm dropping it, and if someone sells it to me well, maybe I'll come back to it in the future.
