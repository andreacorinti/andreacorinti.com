This post is first of all a test, so bear with me!

From now on, under some of the posts on this blog, you'll find the replies from Bluesky, just like the ones from the fediverse that already show up under my Italian posts. To try it out I needed a real post, and filling it with lorem ipsum felt way too sad, like talking to a Morrowind NPC with nothing but "latest rumors" and "little secret" in the dialogue menu, so I figured I might as well write down the whole reasoning behind it (which, as usual, got a bit out of hand).

## The Mastodon/Bluesky dilemma

I've been in the fediverse for years now, and for a while I was even a moderator, so I've seen my fair share of how the sausage gets made.

I really believe in the principles behind it: open source software, no billionaire owner who wakes up one morning and renames everything after a letter of the alphabet, and no algorithm deciding what I should be angry about today!

The problem is that Mastodon, as a place to actually hang out, doesn't really win me over, for a thousand reasons: it's small, it grows slowly, finding interesting stuff is a side quest with no map and no fairy yelling _"Hey, listen!"_ at you, and let's be honest, the Italian corner of it can be pretty exhausting.

![The Mastodon mascot walking away with a bindle in the colours of the Italian flag](/img/mastodon-it.jpg)

Bluesky has more people and way more variety, sure, but it's still a company funded by investors who will want their money back sooner or later, and its active users have been going down for a while now...

So I stay on Mastodon a bit like those couples who stay together "for the kids", except here the kids are open source and decentralisation! Is it love? Kind of. Is it a little sad? Yeah, kind of that too.

## The English problem

![The British and American flags merged into one](/img/english.jpg)

Then there's the other dilemma, the one that bugs me the most.

Writing in Italian means talking to an audience that's already small to begin with and that, on average, doesn't read that much. Add the fact that I'm a nerd who writes about video games (often old ones), Linux and metal music, and it's already a small miracle that anyone reads these posts at all! (hi, by the way, both of you!)

English opens the door to a much bigger audience, sure, but it's also an endless ocean of content where it's ten times easier to get lost: more potential readers, yes, but also millions more blogs, newsletters and posts fighting over the same five minutes of their attention. Jumping from a small pond into the open sea doesn't automatically mean more people will find you, it might just mean you sink faster!

And then the web is already drowning in English: every Italian who switches language online makes Italian on the internet a tiny bit smaller, and I'm not exactly thrilled about being part of that. It's not an abstract worry for me either, since I work on an e-learning platform for people learning Italian as a foreign language, so I know very well that a language needs to be _used_ to stay alive, in messy everyday places like a nerd blog, not kept in a glass case like a rare cartridge nobody dares to plug in.

## So why bother?

The funny thing is that I don't even gain anything from being read! No ads around here, no dreams of fame... at most I'd like to end up in a "good circle" of people where something interesting might come out of it. Okay, put like that it sounds like I'm trying to join some secret lodge, but you get the idea!

As I often write in my Italian posts, I really miss the small old forums and the threads that came out of them, and my blogging comes a bit from there. And no, Discord is not the answer: I've always hated it, long before premium subscriptions and all the other nonsense. The only one I'm still fine with is Telegram, which, to be fair, often does its very best to give me a headache too.

Anyway, writing in English is also a way like any other to keep my English alive, training it by talking about stuff I actually care about, and to run some real experiments with it. Anyone who speaks more than one language and blogs knows that this comes with more than one headache, especially if you don't want to translate every post 1:1: some things (like this one) are meant only for English readers, while others are so full of Italian references that they only make sense to Italians.

Basically, it's all done _"a cazzo di cane"_, as René Ferretti would say (if you're a native English speaker I honestly have no idea what you're getting out of this one, sorry!).

## The compromise, for now

So for now most posts here will stay in Italian, and the ones that might interest people outside Italy will come out in English too, but not necessarily as twin copies of each other. Not perfect, but I can live with it without feeling like a traitor to either side!

Actually, now that I think about it, there's another option on the table: posting in Italian on Mastodon and in English on Bluesky, with each language getting its own comment section here on the blog. Italian posts with fediverse comments, English posts with Bluesky comments, everyone in their own lane.

Not a bad idea, honestly, but it sounds just a tiny bit byzantine, I'll admit. Oh well, until I come up with something better, let's give it a try!

So, being an English post, this one gets Bluesky comments only, and it's also the actual test: reply to it on Bluesky and your reply will show up down there. If it doesn't, well... at least I'll know something's broken!
