_This article was first published in Italian on April 14, 2025._

**Part one:** for the first week impressions, see the [February post](/posts/miyoo-mini-plus-review/)

Well: after two months and 100 hours of play, I think I can talk about this magnificent little gadget with a bit more knowledge of the facts.

My stay in Italy is lasting longer than planned, and in the last few months I often found myself on a train heading to:

- Rome
- Genoa
- Bologna

always with the dreadful Como - Milan commuter leg in between.

I don't usually carry backpacks or any luggage apart from my suitcase of the moment, and I find it hard to explain to my past self (very hesitant about buying a little console like this) how much it changes things, gaming-wise, to carry PS1, SNES and so on in your pocket, taking up roughly the same space as the old Game Boy Color.

The result?

My Steam Deck has been switched off for two months, and I feel a bit guilty (very much like a cheating husband), also because the Deck has plenty of games that the Miyoo could never run...

But hey:

### What I'm playing

- Three Castlevanias. I finished Harmony of Dissonance for the first time, replayed all of Symphony of the Night for the umpteenth time, and I'm trying Dawn of Sorrow (luckily, despite the dual screen, it's one of those DS games the Miyoo can handle)

- yet another replay of Final Fantasy IX (other chapters will follow)

- Tetris - Rosy Retrospection

- Gran Turismo 2

- Mario Kart DS

plus various experiments, including [the half disaster I made with the Final Fantasy III NES saves](https://livellosegreto.it/@xabacadabra/114177964565392393) (in Italian)

### Alternatives & competition

I'm quite intrigued by the **Miyoo Flip** (gorgeous to look at), but I'd use it to play more or less the same stuff, and it's less pocketable:

sure, it seems it can emulate the Nintendo 64 too, but for me that's a bit "too little" to justify another Miyoo that isn't that different from the Mini Plus in terms of games.

Much "chunkier" stuff like the **Retroid Pocket 5** is a different story. I'm mostly considering it for my brother (who's rightly very attached to the PS2)... but even there, maybe another Deck would be a better buy, since portability is pretty relative in that case and there's the huge Steam catalogue, which is nothing to sneeze at.

### Bottom line

We'll see in the coming months!

Time for gaming is what it is, and sooner or later I'd like to dust off the poor Deck, but replaying or discovering old glories is still what I love most about today's gaming world, with all due respect to the few new releases that still make my mouth water a little.
