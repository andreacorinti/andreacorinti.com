_This article was first published in Italian on February 24, 2025._

Since I'm back in Italy for a while, at the beginning of February I had a nostalgia attack and dug out my ancient, dusty and totally beaten-up (but more or less working) Game Boy Color.

Well, you know the monkey from 2001: A Space Odyssey?

![Kubrick's monkey](https://i1.wp.com/badbooksgoodtimes.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/2001-monkey-gif.gif)

### Steam Deck

Next October my Steam Deck[^deck] turns three, and the thought blows my mind.

It feels like I bought it yesterday, and yet I've ground through quite a few games on it:

_Zelda: Breath of the Wild, The Last Story, Metaphor: ReFantazio, Final Fantasy XII, Baldur's Gate 3, Vampire Survivors_...

I never counted the hours, but they're a whole lot.

Also because, since I got it, **it has been my one and only gaming machine.**

I uninstalled Steam and every emulator from the laptop I use for work (and for writing posts like this one), giving all my gaming love to Valve's wonderful machine.

[^deck]: in Italian I always think of the Deck as a "she", maybe because to me it's a PSP on steroids, even though it's really a laptop in disguise.

### The monkey

The funny thing?

I thought that sooner or later I'd give in to some new PC or console, finding some next-gen compromise for the new big releases, especially the very "cinematic" ones.

The truth?

There are games that tickle me, sure, but I already know I couldn't really enjoy them, between the proverbial thousand things to do and my gypsy-like wandering around Europe.

More often than not, like it happened with [Final Fantasy XVI](/posts/Final-Fantasy-XVI-noia) (in Italian), the rule is _"I'll try it at a friend's place and then watch it on YouTube like a movie"_.

Instead, playing Pokémon Red again, and above all holding the Game Boy form factor in my hands, gave me a real jolt.

You'll say:

_ok, but you can emulate the whole world on the Steam Deck_

True. And in fact I replayed all of Pokémon Crystal there not long ago, enjoying it a lot...

But as Auron, master of cool one-liners, would put it: this is a whole other story.

### Mechdiy

I bought the Miyoo plus a 128 GB SD card directly from the great guys at [Mechdiy](https://mechdiy.com/), and I can only say good things about them:

1. If you buy the Miyoo from AliExpress or Amazon, chances are you'll end up with a crappy SD card that's only good for the bin

2. You can buy a good SD card separately (Mechdiy sent me a SanDisk), install OnionOS and so on, but having everything ready out of the box is a real blessing

Sadly they don't pay me to advertise them, eh, but if you can find a discount code you'll spend a bit more and save yourself a few headaches, especially if you're not a tinkerer (but even if you are)

I tried a bit of everything, but most of my hours so far went here:

speaking of which:

in its tools, OnionOS also has a nice tracker that shows which games you spend the most time on!

For the rest, everything runs perfectly!

In my tests the only slight slowdown I noticed was with **Vagrant Story** (nothing serious, eh, and surely fixable with some tinkering).

Then there's the Nintendo DS question: with two screens squeezed into one tiny display, many games don't give you much of an experience unless the second screen is barely used (but that's a cross this console always carries around): Mario Kart DS, for example, is fabulous with the second screen minimised in the top right corner.

### Rivals

![Game Boy Color, Miyoo Mini Plus and Steam Deck](/img/miyoo-mini-plus.jpg)

Why did I pick the Miyoo Mini Plus among the gazillion retro handhelds out there?

First, because I read pretty much everywhere that the controls are top quality (and I can confirm it, even though I don't know how the competition does) and, thanks to its huge popularity, **OnionOS and the community behind it give you great support when you have problems or go hunting for features.**

But above all, it's the ideal compromise for the _purest_ portability, the closest thing to the Game Boy: no analog sticks and no horizontal screen, but the perfect form factor to fit in your jeans pocket (plus a very long battery life that bigger consoles can only dream of).

As I said, having the Deck for "chunkier" games, this fits my needs, although I confess I was very tempted by the Miyoo Flip (maybe I'll think about it in the future).

On a more trivial note, on tiny screens like these analog sticks or other extras would be pretty superfluous for me, since going beyond the PS1 wouldn't do me much good anyway.

And the "plain" Miyoo Mini?

I've never had one in my hands, so I'm going by the specs: the heart is the same (same processor, so the same games run), but the Plus has a bigger screen (3.5 inches versus 2.8), a larger battery and Wi-Fi, which comes in handy for OnionOS and RetroAchievements.

The base Mini, on the other hand, is even tinier and more pocketable: if you really want the smallest possible format, she's the one. For the way I play, I'm keeping those extra inches, thank you very much.

### Conclusions

If you often travel by train / bus / subway / plane and so on, this little console is a real marvel, especially for curious and nostalgic retrogamers.
