Back in February 2025 I wrote a long and pretty grumpy post, in Italian, about **content warnings** on Livello Segreto, the Italian Mastodon server for gamers and nerds where I used to hang out (and where for a while I was even a moderator). Short version: politics kept landing without a CW in a place born to talk about video games, and I kept being the broken record asking people to use one.

Now I'm on Bluesky, where there's no CW culture at all... and guess what? The problem didn't go away, it just moved somewhere else!

So here's the remix, for an international audience this time, plus a part I never wrote back then: about money, and about how decentralised the fediverse really is.

## Escapism isn't ignorance

Something that, for years, a lot of people just don't seem to get:

**wanting to talk about video games, comics or music doesn't mean we spend 24 hours a day ignoring the world we live in.**

If I post about a good album it doesn't mean I skipped the news this morning, or that I don't have an opinion on what's happening in Gaza.

The point is that **nobody walks into a comic shop or an arcade expecting a political rally.** If anything, maybe they walk in exactly because they just _came_ from one, and they'd like to recharge the batteries for an hour, focusing on nice things that make them feel better.

Johan Huizinga wrote a whole book about it, _Homo Ludens_, in 1938 (not exactly a chill year in Europe, eh?): play isn't a distraction from culture, it's one of the things culture is made of. If people read Huizinga half as much as they read the news, maybe they'd have a vague idea of how important the _ludic_ is for human beings, for heaven's sake.

Recreation matters even when things are bad.

Actually, _especially_ when things are bad.

## Human RSS feeds

We can't know how people live their day offline: how much they already argued, for work or for passion, about the same topics that get thrown in their face the moment they open an app to rest a bit.

And maybe what they find is a timeline full of links to newspapers they'd like to set on fire, shared by **people who turned into human RSS feeds**, not reading the context they're in nor the people around them. Healthy carriers of unconscious clickbait, more interested in sharing _their_ stuff (which often isn't even theirs) than in reading anybody else.

The same people who then tell you you're "burying your head in the sand" (sigh).

Not everybody, of course! But you know the type.

## Online outrage doesn't change much

This is a fixation of mine and I always bring up the same article: [Political Hobbyists Are Ruining Politics](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2020/01/political-hobbyists-are-ruining-politics/605212/), by Eitan Hersh in _The Atlantic_ (January 2020). His point: lots of people consume politics like a hobby, well informed, very loud, very angry. A bit like being a sports fan: you know every stat, you yell at the TV, but you're not on the pitch.

Now forget what I think for a second, and think about _your_ internet. How it was twenty-five years ago, how much digital activism happened since then, how social media swallowed everything.

**Does the world look better for it?**

To be fair, online activism did good things too! The web helped keep alive and connect squats and social centres, indie and underground scenes, hacktivism... but almost everything that really worked had a _physical_ piece somewhere: a place, a neighbourhood, people in a room. And if those realities made life better for a few people on their block, for me they already did more than all political parties and their social media managers put together.

Here I have to confess something, though:

I used to _believe_ in it. Long before some Italian politicians made a mess of it, I found the idea of **liquid democracy** (people deciding things together online, more or less directly) incredibly fascinating.

Well, **I was wrong.** All the way.

Today I think a small local party branch, the kind with plastic chairs and terrible coffee, can do much more (for better or worse) than tons of digital sophistry that in the end has to play by the rules of today's techno-feudalism. And whoever goes down that road must also be ready to sit and talk with people who voted for the far right, even when they'd rather punch them. It's a job for martyrs, and I really admire whoever does it, especially in some small provincial town, and not on Instagram.

## Occupying every space

Everywhere you can talk about _everything_, and so you end up talking about the same four things, the trending topic of the day eating everything else.

It's the McDonald's in Java that David Bowie talked about:

> I was traveling in Java when the first McDonald's went up: it was like, 'for fuck's sake.' The invasion by any homogenized culture is so depressing, the erection of another Disney World in, say, Umbria, Italy, more so. It strangles the indigenous culture and narrows expression of life.[^bowie]

[^bowie]: David Bowie on "I'm Afraid of Americans", quoted in [_David Bowie: 30 Essential Songs_](https://web.archive.org/web/20210411024605/https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-lists/david-bowie-best-songs-33438/im-afraid-of-americans-1997-3-151698/), Rolling Stone, 11 January 2016.

The idea that no space can stay even a _little_ bit sheltered from something growing everywhere like ivy, a place where you can disconnect and think about something else. And sometimes I start to suspect that this exhausting climate is good for someone, because the more tired people are, the easier it is for them to stop caring and stay home on election day.

## Follow the money

Now the new part, and let me be clear first: I don't want to sound like a conspiracy guy, and even less like a fan of privatising everything.

With newspapers, knowing who the publisher and the sponsors are actually lets me read them without paranoia: I know that paper has its own axe to grind, I adjust for it and take the good stuff (inside there are also lots of great professionals who have to make compromises to pay the rent, but they know their job). When the publisher is "unknown", or it's all about donations and EU funds, which are certainly nice things, Giovanni Falcone's old _follow the money_ gets a lot more complicated.

So I wonder how some of these outfits keep the lights on, how much of it is sincere, disinterested activism (and there's a lot, really) and how much is what I'd call _virtue-washing_, that in the end behaves just like the ugly alternatives.

Take Mastodon, which is the one I know best:

- in Germany its company [lost its charitable status](https://blog.joinmastodon.org/2026/02/administrative-updates/) (February 2026), going from gGmbH to plain **GmbH**; a Belgian non-profit is planned as the new parent, German donations now go through a fiscal sponsor, [WE AID](https://blog.joinmastodon.org/2025/11/mastodon-joins-forces-with-we-aid/), and in the US there's a separate 501(c)(3);
- the founder, Eugen Rochko, stepped down as CEO in November 2025 and, according to [heise](https://heise.de/-11081969), got a **one-off €1 million**, for his work so far and for handing over the Mastodon trademark;
- in 2024, about 96% of its ~€2.2M revenue came from donations, but look closer and a big slice comes from a few **major donors**: Jeff Atwood alone gave $1.5M for the restructuring plus another $100k, then Mozilla Foundation ($100k), Craig Newmark ($50k); on the commercial side there's hosting the European Commission's Mastodon server and a support contract with the German state of Schleswig-Holstein, plus EU grants via NGI/NLnet and a security audit sponsored by the German BSI ([Annual Report 2024](https://joinmastodon.org/reports/Mastodon%20Annual%20Report%202024.pdf));
- in 2026 came a [€614k service agreement](https://blog.joinmastodon.org/2026/04/sovereign-tech-agency-funding/) with the Sovereign Tech Agency, which is public money: it was born as a [subsidiary of SPRIND](https://sovereign.tech/news/stf-becomes-independent-permanent-organization), the German federal agency for breakthrough innovation, and launched by the federal Ministry for Economic Affairs;
- and yes, my memory wasn't lying: Samsung NEXT's Stack Zero Grant is still listed among the contributors on [Mastodon's Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/mastodon), $70,000 since 2019.

None of this means "Mastodon is corrupt", let me be clear!

Most of it is pretty normal stuff for an open source project that wants to survive, and it's all public, which is already more than many can say. The point is that the structure got **complicated**, and figuring out who's pulling the strings (and why) is way harder than with a publisher whose name is printed on the first page.

And Bluesky? Bluesky is a company with investors, a piece of techno-feudalism like the others, and I know it very well. I'm not here to tell you I found the good guys.

## Decentralised, but not too much

Then there's the decentralisation thing.

In 2023, when Meta's Threads was still a rumour called "Project 92", a bunch of fediverse admins signed the [Anti-Meta Fedi Pact](https://fedipact.online/), promising to block any Meta instance before it even showed up. Mastodon's official line was different: in [July 2023](https://blog.joinmastodon.org/2023/07/what-to-know-about-threads/) they called Threads adopting ActivityPub "a clear victory for our cause" and left the choice to each server. Threads started actually federating in [March 2024](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/03/21/networking-traffic/threads-has-entered-the-fediverse/), and mastodon.social, the biggest server of all, never blocked it: today threads.net doesn't appear in its [public list of moderated servers](https://mastodon.social/about).

Which brings me back to a line from my old post:

as soon as the numbers go up, you enter the same league where Bezos, Musk and Zuckerberg play. Maybe fighting to avoid relegation and not for the Champions League, but still.

And it's hard to ignore how much weight **Mastodon** has on the whole ActivityPub world, both as software and as mastodon.social, with all due respect to whoever preaches decentralisation: according to [FediDB](https://fedidb.com/software), about two thirds of the fediverse's monthly active users (654K out of 980K) are on Mastodon servers.

## No saints here

I don't want to be a defeatist, but one thing is sure: **looking for purity is pure utopia.**

We have to deal with reality, with our own hypocrisies and contradictions, possibly without hiding them, and behave like aware and decent people without pretending to be saints.

Me included, posting this on a network owned by investors, about a network kept alive by a handful of big donors and public money, from a blog that collects comments from both.
