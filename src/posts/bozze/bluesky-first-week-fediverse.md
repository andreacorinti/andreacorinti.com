A few days ago I wrote about [Mastodon, Bluesky and the language problem](/posts/mastodon-bluesky-english/), and I ended it with a half-baked idea: Italian on Mastodon, English on Bluesky. Well, I actually did it.

For the past few days I've been using **Bluesky** a lot, and I mean _a lot_, trying to build an English-speaking corner there, while the **fediverse** (Mastodon, in my case) stays the place where I write in Italian. These are my notes on how it went: setting things up with **Bridgy Fed**, playing with **custom feeds** and **starter packs**, and what growing on Bluesky looks like for someone who's spent years on Mastodon.

If you're coming from Bluesky, hi! If you're coming from the fediverse, hi to you too, and you'll see in a minute why you can read and reply to this as well.

## Setting up: native account, own domain, Bridgy Fed

My account is a native Bluesky one, not bridged from somewhere else: it's [@andreacorinti.com](https://bsky.app/profile/andreacorinti.com), with this very domain as the handle.

To connect the two worlds there's [Bridgy Fed](https://fed.brid.gy/), and the nice thing about it is that it's opt-in, in both directions: nobody gets bridged without asking for it. To bridge a Bluesky profile to the fediverse you just follow `@ap.brid.gy`, and that's it. Now people on Mastodon & co. can find me as `@andreacorinti.com@bsky.brid.gy`, and their replies come back to Bluesky.

Then there's the blog: as I explained in the [previous post](/posts/mastodon-bluesky-english/), replies from both Bluesky and the fediverse already show up under my posts, so the circle is closed. More or less (see the not-so-great parts below).

## What actually worked

Short version: niche topics, images, and talking to people.

The post that did best by far was a thread about the look of PC-98 games. After that came a screenshot of my Linux Mint desktop, a grid with my games of the year from 1991 to 2026, and a thread about the Nobuo Uematsu concerts I've been to. Not exactly mainstream stuff, and that's kind of the point: on Bluesky, niche doesn't automatically mean invisible (more on that in a second).

But what worked even better than posting was _replying_. Jumping into other people's conversations, especially small and mid-sized accounts, brought me more followers than any post did. Real conversations, the kind I keep saying I miss from the old forums.

Alt text on every single image, too. On Bluesky it's part of the shared culture, so I went along with it from day one.

And then there was [Knowember](https://robertbirming.com/knowember/), Robert Birming's November blogging event. Writing [my answers](/posts/knowember-2026/) put me in touch with other bloggers, which is exactly the kind of "good circle" I was hoping to find.

In a few days my follower count went from about fifteen to about forty. Not exactly influencer numbers, but I'll take it!

## Custom feeds, trends and the algorithm question

This is the biggest difference from Mastodon, and the one I find most interesting.

**Custom feeds**: anyone can create a themed feed (Linux, JRPGs, retro gaming, IndieWeb…) that picks up posts based on keywords and hashtags. So if I write a post that mentions "JRPG" or a specific game series, it can end up in one of those feeds all by itself, even if nobody follows me. On Mastodon, finding interesting stuff is a side quest with no map; here, custom feeds are more like fast travel points.

**Trends** work differently too: they're not based on hashtags, but on groups of posts using the same words.

Then there's the algorithm. The For You feed learns mostly from your likes, while on Discover you can tell it "show more" or "show less" of something. A while ago I admitted [(in Italian)](/posts/di-nuovo-su-mastodon-social/) that I wouldn't mind a feed tailored to me, as long as there were no weird sponsorships and no data sold to the highest bidder. On Bluesky, at least for now, there's no paid promotion at all.

A couple more things I didn't have on Mastodon:

- **starter packs**, lists of accounts you can follow in one go, which are really handy to get into a niche;
- **threadgates**, which let whoever posts decide who can reply.

## The not-so-great parts

It's not all fast travel and alt text, of course.

First, follow-for-follow and spam. Lots of it: "lofi girl" accounts, authors following 90,000 people, plain bots. At some point I did some cleaning and removed the spam followers. I'd rather have a few real followers than a big number full of ghosts.

Then, a funny one for a guy who wrote a whole post about the language problem: you can't change a post's language tag after publishing it. One of my posts ended up tagged as "it" by mistake, and the only way to fix it was to delete it and post it again.

The US side of things is huge, too. Timing matters (the Italian evening is the American afternoon), and Discover was full of American politics, which I had to tame with a lot of "show less".

Last, the bridge and deletions. I deleted a post on Bluesky, but replies from the fediverse kept coming in after that: the copy was still around over there, and from Bluesky I couldn't reply anymore. A bit like an NPC who keeps talking about a quest you already abandoned.

## Bluesky vs the fediverse, for now

I've already written about why Mastodon doesn't really win me over as a place to hang out, and why I stay anyway (the "together for the kids" thing, where the kids are open source and decentralisation), so I won't repeat it [here](/posts/mastodon-bluesky-english/).

Funny thing: I found Knowember through Bluesky, even though Robert Birming posts from a Mastodon server. More than four years on the fediverse, and our paths never crossed once. That's my main issue with it: without some kind of discovery, good people just stay invisible.

To be fair, I still like Mastodon's sober interface, and search got a lot better with version 5.0 [(in Italian)](/posts/di-nuovo-su-mastodon-social/). So for now the split works: Italian over there, English here, and the blog in the middle collecting replies from both.

So, that's week one. If you think I'm doing it all wrong, replies from Bluesky and from the fediverse both end up right under this post, so pick your side. Or both, like me.
