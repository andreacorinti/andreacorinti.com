As mentioned here and there in previous chapters, the Fediverse is a group of many decentralized, and mostly (though not exclusively) open-source social platforms, joined together by the ActivityPub protocol — the same one used, just to be clear, by Meta's **Threads**. **Bluesky**, on the other hand, runs on its own protocol, the AT Protocol, although bridges like Bridgy Fed let the two worlds talk to each other anyway.

## I don't understand what you mean by *"decentralized social media"*

it's a concept that's more complex to explain than to actually try out:

imagine free platforms that anyone can freely tinker with, where any community of tech enthusiasts can build their own custom Twitter, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, or Reddit…

But above all, *all* of these platforms can talk to each other, so you can use them all with a single account!

## So I could comment on a video on some pseudo-YouTube using my account from some kind of X?!

Exactly!

For example, I use [GoToSocial](https://gotosocial.org/) for a small personal instance: through my account I can interact with the whole Fediverse regardless of platform or context.

Now, to be clear:

since these are tools available to anyone, you can find all kinds of things there, for better or worse.

Behind Donald Trump's *Truth Social*, for instance, is Mastodon — the most famous of these programs, originally spread partly thanks to anarchist and communist collectives who have decidedly little in common with Trump.

## Ok, got it. But going back to what we were saying before: how exactly are these Fediverse platforms supposed to be more virtuous when it comes to dopamine addiction?

In part, some of the same issues just carry right over — no point denying it.

But there's one really important distinction:

in his excellent *Irresistible: The Rise of Addictive Technology and the Business of Keeping Us Hooked*, professor Adam Alter points out that these technologies aren't inherently good or bad, but that the whole addiction problem comes from the way **companies design them for mass consumption:**

Algorithms, engagement, advertising, data sales, and so on.

> It's easy to look back at how little Freud and Pemberton understood of cocaine with a sense of superiority. We teach our children that cocaine is dangerous, and it's hard to believe that experts considered the drug a panacea only a century ago. But perhaps our sense of superiority is misplaced. Just as cocaine charmed Freud and Pemberton, today we're enamored of technology. We're willing to overlook its costs for its many gleaming benefits: for on-demand entertainment portals, car services, and cleaning companies; Facebook and Twitter; Instagram and Snapchat; Reddit and Imgur; Buzzfeed and Mashable; Gawker and Gizmodo; online gambling sites, Internet video platforms, and streaming music hubs; hundred-hour work weeks, power naps, and four-minute gym workouts; and the rise of a new breed of obsessions, compulsions, and addictions that barely existed during the twentieth century.
>
> And then there's the social world of the modern teen.[^20]

But if behind a piece of software there isn't some giant corporation with a vested interest in fueling addiction, but instead free code maintained by people we can actually trust, well…

The situation changes radically.

Sure, the risk of addiction doesn't vanish, but at least it's not being cynically incentivized by companies interested in using us as batteries.

## But come on, nobody does anything for nothing. I don't believe that somewhere in this whole Fediverse there's not someone raking in money!

Actually, a bit of healthy suspicion is well warranted:

I myself have more than a few doubts about **Mastodon and mastodon.social**, the main piece of software and the largest instance in the Fediverse, run by the foundation headed by **Eugen Rochko**, the developer and creator of Mastodon itself.

If you want to know more, I'll point you to [this post on my blog](https://www.andreacorinti.com/posts/ita/mastodon%20e%20fediverso/) and [this other one from Fedi.Tips](https://fedi.tips/its-a-really-bad-idea-to-join-a-big-server/)

it's basically the same conversation we could have about Linux and its distributions: not everything is as virtuous as it looks, but at least we're not talking about monstrous techno-feudal giants, which by now increasingly resemble the infamous *zaibatsu* William Gibson describes in his cyberpunk novels.

> Imagine an alien, Fox said, who's come here to identify the planet's dominant form of intelligence. The alien has s look, then chooses. What do you think he picks? I probably shrugged. The zaibatsus, Fox said, the multinationals. The blood of a zaibatsu is information, not people. The structure is independent of the individual lives that comprise it. Corporation as life form. Not the Edge lecture again, I said. [^21]

## Fine, but nobody will ever be able to convince the masses to migrate to a messy, motley Fediverse, especially without the economic interest of those famous big companies.

> It is so shocking to find out how many people do not believe that they can learn, and how many more believe learning to be difficult. [^22]

True, and in fact I'm reasonably convinced the Fediverse will always remain a relatively small environment…

But I don't think that's a bad thing — quite the opposite:

it's a return to the internet communities of the late '90s and early 2000s, with small numbers and a more mindful, more *human* use of the web.

Maybe it's pure utopia that'll disappear within a few years, but over the last three, I have to say I've never once regretted my presence in the Fediverse… And I can't say the same about any other social environment.
