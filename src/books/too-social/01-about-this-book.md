I want to start by making one thing clear right away:

this little book is not *ethically fanatical*.

Are Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Twitter and so on ethically questionable, as well as responsible for some of the disastrous phenomena that have swept through our society over the last few decades?

I'd say yes, absolutely:

without dragging in one of the many studies on the subject (I recommend [Tiktok's enshittification](https://pluralistic.net/2023/01/21/potemkin-ai/#hey-guys) by Cory Doctorow), it's enough to take a look at the documentary *The Social Dilemma* by Jeff Orlowski, which came out in 2020 and so was conceived and filmed well before the various lockdowns that certainly contributed to the recent wave of *hyper-digitalization*.

However, this book won't go into the details of the issue, and above all **it doesn't want to lecture anyone:**

first of all because the person writing this has used pretty much all of these social media (I'm still on Instagram) and, second, because given the choice between a Savonarola and a Lorenzo the Magnificent, I generally tend to prefer the latter.

Last but not least, I believe any moralizing rant tends to achieve the opposite of its intended effect, and mostly ends up being a way of putting on *airs of purity* rather than actually pushing for real change.

## So what should I expect from the pages that follow?

A reflection on Time, and how and where we decide to spend it.

If you've read [*Too Much Gaming!*](https://www.andreacorinti.com/projects/giochi-troppo/) (an essay about how many slices of life we dedicate to video games), let's say you're now looking at its little brother.

With the difference that about video games I could say plenty of wonderful things, while about social media... not quite so many, really.

## So it's the usual sermon about how bad social media is?

No, or at least not exactly.

I'll suggest an exercise straight out of a new-age guru's playbook (forgive me):

ask yourself these questions.

- Why am I on social media?
- How much time do I spend on it?
- How much, and what, do I get back in return?

Close your eyes, and try to give yourself honest answers.

Done?

Good!

Before we reason about how much we're spied on, about how **we hand over our data and even our most intimate information to mega-corporations ready to resell it to target us with ads based on our tastes**, I think we should start from these three questions.

In short: before looking at the whole global picture, let's try to limit ourselves to the *personal* one — which is nonetheless not a simple exercise at all.
