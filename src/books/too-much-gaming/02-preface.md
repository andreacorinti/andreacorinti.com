**By Marco Bortone**

"You play too much!"

How many times have we heard that line from our parents, or from whatever other figure in our lives who, no, just couldn't understand what gaming meant for US? I think I can speak for every one of you: too many times, and we never listened. Not out of some lack of responsibility — homework to do, exams to prepare for, work to get done — no, simply because we wanted to be the ones setting the pace, because WE knew when enough was enough, how far to push ourselves into entertainment in those fantastic virtual worlds before returning to the duties of real life. And, however much my own experience looks back to an era when the perception of video games was still different from today's, younger people are surely facing something totally similar yet different at the same time. Either way, nothing written in this "Too Much Gaming!" loses its usefulness or relevance.

While those of us edging into (or already firmly in) our forties have had our experience and awareness — or simply enough water under the bridge — lead us, at least once, to wonder "Am I Gaming Too Much?", for today's generations this kind of moment is becoming rarer and rarer, given how gaming is now gearing up to enter a whole new era of meta-virtual entertainment. Either way, what's really NEEDED isn't to take the whole "playing too much" issue as some absolute truth, but at least to reflect on it, to try asking yourself to what extent the time devoted to this activity is entertainment, culture, passion, a desire to escape — and how much of it is subtle conditioning or a sweet placebo for something else. After all, whether we're talking about our daily duties or our daily entertainment, knowing how to manage and pace yourself is essential, but above all it's about understanding — what do we really want? How do we really want to use OUR TIME?

Because that's exactly what this is about. Limited time, limited resources, limited choices. The foundation of human life, without which none of this would probably make any sense. The sands of life running through an hourglass that can't be turned back. The need to carefully choose what to do and what to let go. The twenty-four hours that are never enough. But careful: IF the person reading these lines of mine happens to be some future, superior generation of human adolescents blessed with eternal life, then everything changes! Forget my words, don't give a damn: playing, "video gaming," is something fantastic, fulfilling, and wonderfully disorienting all at once — no doubt about that. Getting to live a different life every time, feeling yourself inside new worlds, new realities — simulated or not — maybe interacting with other human players, reaching ever greater levels of immersion, or maybe simply imagining adventures and landscapes that our eyes barely brush against but our minds are already weaving for us... nothing more to say, it's the modern form of entertainment. And maybe in your case it will have reached capabilities and possibilities unimaginable to me. In that case, IF your biological clock is NOT violently ticking away inside your skull making you feel guilty for every moment perhaps WASTED on something you're not entirely sure about, something that might not even truly satisfy you, leave something inside you, change your damn existence, or even just give you a "pure," "genuine" moment free of that already-seen, already-heard, already-felt feeling — then no, relax, go right ahead, keep at it, in fact do even more, and play Too Much for us poor, mortal twenty-first-century humans too.

Isn't that exactly what our grandparents would think about all this? Generations who (literally) fought a World War, who went hungry, who didn't even know if they'd survive to the following month. I realize I might have drifted off track — actually, I'm sure of it, I got carried away and, as usual, lost my train of thought. I completely lost track of time — space itself seemed to melt together with the millions of pixels on the screen I was looking at and the dozens of keys I was pressing, and I found myself completely immersed in myself, somehow stepping outside my own physical body. Like in those (worst) sci-fi movies, where the protagonist enters a trance (a coma?) and their soul rises up to watch their own body from above. All of this happened while I was right there, writing for you and, at the same time, for myself. Isn't that the best possible description of getting completely lost in a video game and, at the same time, finding yourself wondering: haven't I gone too far? Am I not wasting time? Do I REALLY want to collect every single piece of that armor set? Do I really care about unlocking the secret ending after 500 hours of gameplay? And why does this achievement want me to kill ten thousand mobs that all fight with the exact same moveset? Don't I really have anything better to do today?

And just like in that movie, there we are, shouting at our own physical body that maybe it's time to stop, that maybe we need to come back to ourselves and find the right line between dream and reality, especially when the dream risks looking like a monotonous, flat imitation of the reality we could actually be living firsthand. My God, our grandparents walked home on foot after the war, and here we are playing Fortnite in a goofy cartoon-character skin! Ok, message received, my potato spirit returns to my body, there's just a moment of disorientation, then everything comes back into focus and it's clear again why I'm here, why writing this preface felt like absolutely the right thing to do.

Because I too, like the author of this book, have asked myself many times how to best budget my own time spent on gaming as entertainment — how to avoid repeating certain patterns that start to look the same every time, or spending hours on something specifically designed to fool me and my own desire to escape. HE, though, went further: he studied the topic, discovered a whole fascinating world of people who had asked themselves these same questions, each with their own answer and experience. We could say that Andrea Corinti, known online as Xabaras — gamer, blogger, forum regular (is that even a word?), cosplayer, digital pirate (and otherwise) — did his homework. If my own brief rant on the topic mostly came from an off-the-cuff reflection meant to tickle the imagination and curiosity of anyone used to gaming, the way this book tackles the subject and tries to dig into it, jumping from one topic to another the way an RPG player jumps between levels (still trying to tease you here, I admit), is the fruit of work and research that could only come from someone who, like the author of this text, is curious, passionate, and above all never settles for their own reasoning, but seeks it out and tests it, at times almost obsessively, by going and listening to what others have to say too.

The topic, after all, absolutely deserves to be handled this way. Video games get talked about either too much and "badly" (thinking of how experts and enthusiasts in the field indulge in constant self-congratulation without ever showing a shred of critical spirit) or too little and by total ignorance, which ends up pushing kids away from any alternative way of engaging with the medium (and here I'm thinking of the ridiculous attempts by the press, politicians, and the self-righteous to condemn video games for their violence and lack of educational value). You'll find neither of those things in this book, just the genuine critical spirit of a (former?) player and enthusiast, who takes a small step to the side to look at the issue from a different angle and discovers that, standing right there looking in that same direction, there's other people too, looking a bit stunned and a little bit hooked.

And you? What does this have to do with you? Oh, come on... here we go... we're here! This is your chance to learn a little more about gaming, to move on to the next stage, to pick up some information you can make your own and use however you see fit — or simply to have a story about that crazy old guy who criticized your favorite pastime. But here we are, you're here, you have the chance to do it, to set your own pace, your own way, and finally gain real awareness of what you truly want. This time, it won't be your mother yelling at you to turn off the console — it'll be YOU, thinking to yourself that, yeah, deep down maybe...

"You play too much!"
