This appendix is dedicated to all the non-gamers out there.

Besides explaining some terms used in this book, you'll find a few other more or less "gaming-flavored" words, so that the next time you hear them in your kids' or grandkids' conversations, you'll have some idea what they're talking about.

**Anime:** a term used internationally to refer to Japanese animated cartoons. As with manga (see below), in Japan the word actually covers animation as a whole, regardless of country of origin.

**Graphic adventure:** usually (though not exclusively) "point and click," meaning played almost entirely with a mouse, this is a video game genre with a strongly narrative focus. Adventures are built around puzzles and exploration, with a heavy emphasis on dialogue, writing, and characters. The genre evolved out of old text adventures, themselves related to gamebooks.

**Battle Royale:** an online video game mode where players face off against each other, with the winner being the last one standing (see also PVP): *Fortnite* is currently the leading example of the genre. The term comes from the 2000 film of the same name by Kinji Fukasaku, itself based on the novel by Koushun Takami.

**Boomer:** short for baby boomer, the generation born between 1946 and 1964. Thanks to the "Ok Boomer" meme, the term often transcends actual age, being used dismissively to call out a certain conservatism tied to digital illiteracy: interestingly, baby boomers themselves also include the very first developers and scholars of video games as a medium.

**Discord:** messaging software born within the gaming community, now used by millions of people to communicate even outside of a gaming context.

**Gamification**: the use of game-like elements in non-game contexts such as social media, schools, or workplaces. It usually involves accumulating points, levels, status badges, or other measurable stats.

**Generation Alpha**: the most recently catalogued generation, covering those born from 2010 to the mid-2020s.

**Generation X**: covers those born between 1965 and 1980. This generation represents the pioneers of gaming as mass entertainment, having lived through the *golden age* of the '70s and the console/PC gaming boom of the '80s and '90s.

**Grinding:** a term that, in gaming, refers to repetitive actions aimed at growing your character, usually through accumulating *experience points* (see relevant entry).

**Hack'n'slash:** a genre of combat-focused video games generally centered on melee weapons. A classic hack'n'slash is *Diablo*, an action-focused RPG.

**Hikikomori:** literally "to be secluded" in Japanese, this identifies an extreme form of asociality and isolation, often tied to agoraphobia or other forms of social anxiety.

**Loot box**: literally a "box of loot," this is the main source of rewards in a lot of video games. Its contents generally vary by rarity, using mechanics that lean close to gambling.

**Manga**: while in Japan the term "manga" refers to comics in general, the rest of the world, much like with anime, uses it to specifically mean Japanese comics.

**Millennial:** or Generation Y, a term for those born between 1981 and 1995. The term has often been misused to refer to people born from 2000 onward (see *Zoomer*). Millennials are generally considered the first digital natives, meaning they were born and raised in close contact with new information technology.

**MMO**: short for *Massively Multiplayer Online*, referring to any online video game accessed simultaneously by a large number of players. There are various types, and one defining trait is the strong community aspect among players.

**MMORPG:** short for *Massively Multiplayer Online Role-Playing Game*, specifically referring to online RPGs (see also RPG). Hugely popular starting in the early 2000s with the *World of Warcraft* phenomenon, it's one of the genres most closely tied to gaming addiction.

**Mod**: short for *modification*, in video games this refers to a variation or additional content created by fans. Several developers, like Bethesda, have long encouraged this by providing players with dedicated modding tools.

**MOBA:** short for *Multiplayer Online Battle Arena*, referring to real-time strategy games played online, with *League of Legends* very likely being the genre's biggest example.

**Nerd:** quoting Wikipedia's definition: "a term used to describe someone with a certain aptitude for technology and activities requiring real dedication, while also tending to be solitary and less inclined toward socializing. The term originated as an insult, but has since been reclaimed in some circles as a source of pride and group identity." The term is often interchangeable with *Geek*, and for several years now people have routinely talked about the rise of nerd culture in entertainment.

**Otaku**: born in Japan in the '80s, in the West this generally identifies the most hardcore anime and manga fans. In its country of origin, it started out as a genuine insult, often closely tied to social isolation (see also: *hikikomori*). During the 2020s, in the US (and consequently online), the slang terms *weeb* and *weeaboo* spread, carrying much of that same derogatory weight as the original Japanese term.

**Beat 'em up:** a genre of fighting games such as *Street Fighter*, *Tekken*, or *Soul Calibur*. Originally a classic arcade genre, today it remains hugely relevant in online competitive gaming.

**Experience points**: also called XP / EXP, in RPGs (see below) and plenty of other games, these represent a character's growth — accumulating them lets a character level up and become more powerful. They're usually earned by defeating enemies or completing certain quests.

**PVE:** short for *Player Versus Environment*, referring to games or modes where the player faces off against AI opponents.

**PVP:** short for *Player Versus Player*, referring to games or modes where the player faces off against other human players.

**Reddit:** a website launched in 2005, an aggregator of news and discussions organized into a variety of themed *subreddits*, comparable to old-school internet forums, Discord servers, or Facebook groups.

**RPG:** short for *Role Playing Game*. The term originally referred to tabletop games (*Dungeons & Dragons*, *Vampire: The Masquerade*, etc.) and is commonly considered one of the video game genres that demands the most time and dedication.

**Skill:** literally "ability," referring to a character's specific abilities. Interestingly, the term also gets used informally to describe a skilled, competent player, or more broadly, someone with specific expertise.

**Skin:** literally "skin," in video games this refers to a character's look and appearance. In a lot of games, like *Fortnite*, skins are paid content.

**Shooter:** can be first-person (FPS – first person shooter) or third-person (TPS). As the name suggests, this genre is essentially built around shooting enemies, who can be either AI or other human players.

**Steam, GOG, Epic Store:** video game distribution platforms, essentially online stores for buying PC games.

**Streamer:** an entertainer who broadcasts video on online platforms, generally YouTube (hence "YouTuber") and Twitch. A game-streamer specifically broadcasts their own gaming sessions.

**Zoomer:** or Generation Z, referring to those born between 1996 and 2010. The term is a kind of counterpart to *Baby Boomer*, and became widespread during the pandemic thanks to the constant use of Zoom and similar software (Skype, FaceTime, Teams).
